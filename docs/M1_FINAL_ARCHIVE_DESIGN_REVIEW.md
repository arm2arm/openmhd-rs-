## Supported design and observed scope

The sequence in `M1_FINAL_ARCHIVE_PLAN.md` is sound: immutable evidence first; a separate AI reviewer pins its manifest and closes a sibling reviewer inventory; the coordinator resolves findings and writes scoped acceptance only after the actual gates and review; a final outer closure pins both nested manifests and the acceptance record. No circular review/evidence hash is needed. This workflow does not yet implement the final outer verifier, and this review does not supply acceptance.

The collector requires a fresh destination, complete terminal inputs and a passing registered verdict before creating output. It invokes the unchanged six independent reduction reports (medium, mean, waves, physics boost, shock, vortex), not numerical evolution. The physics collector uses `outputs/raw/<case-name>/<filename>` from each fresh workflow and verifies actual bytes against that workflow's manifest and run record. Its four workers are transport concurrency, with no numerical submission/start/stop path. Copying an already downloaded fresh artifact is legitimate; campaign-A hash equality is recorded after fresh acquisition/reduction and does not authorize substituting A bytes. Partial output is retained on subsequent failure.

The arithmetic coverage is correctly intended as 48+24+112+30=214 cases, 24+12+48+15=99 boost pairs, eight spatial ladders, twelve temporal comparisons, twelve primary shocks and six primary vortices. The 238 consumed inputs are 214 final CSVs plus twelve shock/six vortex geometry files plus six vortex projected-bin files. Neither aggregate count alone nor byte identity to A proves new arithmetic. Both normalizations, geometry/bin integration, state/budget/divergence scopes, loop error/extremum tradeoff and observed temporal dt behavior remain matters for the unchanged reducers and final output reviewer.

The historical Git-blob retention strategy avoids a future M2 working-tree dependency. I independently hashed the selected **237** historical blobs: 226 science/configuration files, one toolchain file and ten external-reference/provenance/license files; every digest matched the submission snapshot. That checks retained identities, not a build. Gzip `mtime=0` and recording both stored and decoded lengths/hashes are appropriate; the verifier checks decompression against those identities. This does not promise byte-identical gzip output across all Python/zlib versions. The archive explicitly leaves large raw inputs outside it, identifiable through receipts/manifests; it must not claim raw inclusion, a standalone build, independent operator execution, paper equivalence, general invariance, or M1 acceptance.
