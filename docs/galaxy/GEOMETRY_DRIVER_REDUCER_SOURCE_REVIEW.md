4. **Supported exact scalar/error arithmetic and conservative uncertainty.** E29–112. The finite word decoder uses significand `(1<<52)|fraction` and exponent `e-1075` for normal exponent bits e, or fraction times `2^-1074` for subnormals. Rational text must equal its reduced canonical Fraction spelling and obey the text-length bound.

   For a reference scalar `f*sqrt(q)`, the reducer checks a positive radicand/nonzero signed factor and ordered same-sign bounds. If f>0 it requires `lo²<=f²q<=hi²`; if f<0 it requires `hi²<=f²q<=lo²`. The sign conditions make these valid containment proofs, rather than merely matching squared magnitudes. The observational nearest word is finite but does not supply the exact target. Independently verifying its correct rounding belongs to the reference source/controls and actual reference provenance.

   For candidate x and target in [a,b], the minimum possible absolute error is `max(a-x,x-b,0)` and the maximum is `max(|x-a|,|x-b|)`. For nonnegative error [e0,e1] and independently positive denominator [d0,d1], `[e0/d1,e1/d0]` encloses the normalized error. Vector squared error sums the squared component bounds; correlations can make this conservative, not falsely narrow. A positive lower denominator is mandatory. No candidate floor, midpoint substitution or tuning is present. `upper<=limit` passes, `lower>limit` fails and every crossing—including lower exactly at the inclusive gate with upper above it—is unresolved. All required observations must pass. These are mathematical enclosure arguments, not evaluated results or a claim that the finite intervals will resolve every case.
